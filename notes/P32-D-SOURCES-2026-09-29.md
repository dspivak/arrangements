## (ii) Mistranslation weights by position and transition/transversion

**Not read: the primary.** Freeland SJ, Hurst LD, "The genetic code is one in a million,"
J. Mol. Evol. 47, 238-248 (1998), doi 10.1007/PL00006381, PMID 9732450. Paywalled at Springer; no
open-access copy was reachable. It is **not cited** as read.

**Read, and used: a reproduction with a locator.**

> Buhrman H, van der Gulik PTS, Klau GW, Schaffner C, Speijer D, Stougie L, "A Realistic Model
> under which the Genetic Code is Optimal," arXiv:1309.4589v1 [q-bio.PE], 18 Sep 2013,
> **section 2, "Methods,"** p. 8, the displayed list of weights, introduced as "the following set
> of weights introduced by Freeland and Hurst [21] which differentiates between transition errors
> (i.e. U to C, C to U, A to G, G to A) and transversion errors and the position where they occur
> in the codon". Reference **[21] = Freeland SJ, Hurst LD, J. Mol. Evol. 47, 238-248 (1998)**.
> Quoted verbatim:
>
> - alpha_{c,c'} = 0.5 if (c, c') is a transversion in the first position or a transition in the
>   second position,
> - alpha_{c,c'} = 0.1 if (c, c') is a transversion in the second position,
> - alpha_{c,c'} = 1 otherwise.

Unfolded into the script's `weight(pos, b1, b2)` layout:

| position | transition | transversion | script, from memory | diff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 1 | 0.5 | (1.0, 0.5) | - |
| 2nd | 0.5 | 0.1 | (0.5, 0.1) | - |
| 3rd | 1 | 1 | (1.0, 1.0) | - |

**Every weight agrees exactly. No differences.**

Two things this source settles and one it does not. It settles the numbers and that they are
Freeland and Hurst's. It does not settle **how they were derived**: Freeland and Hurst's abstract
(read on the Springer landing page and PubMed) says they weight "each base differently, depending
on reported mistranslation biases," and the derivation of 0.5 and 0.1 from those biases is in the
paywalled text. So the weights are sourced; the empirical argument behind them is not, and the
book may not claim it is. The 2013 paper's own gloss is that position weights of this shape
"impl[y] the existence of a tRNA with a triplet anticodon during the process of code evolution."

## Net result of piece 1

**Nothing was wrong.** All 20 polar requirements, all three position/type weight pairs, and all 64
codon assignments in `scripts/genetic_code_stability.py` match the sourced values exactly. The
head-of-file warning "from memory, to be checked" is discharged; the values are now checkable,
though against reproductions rather than the two primaries, neither of which could be read here.

Because the sourced inputs are numerically identical to the from-memory ones, the rerun is a
reproduction, not a new measurement, and cannot move the verdicts. The one genuinely new number
the rerun can produce is the sensitivity check on the 2008 scale. Both are in
`scripts/genetic_code_stability_sourced.py`; results below.

the 2013 paper states them (not by copying the original's table, so the comparison is real) and
`D` from Table I's polar requirements, and **asserts** the rebuilt inputs equal the from-memory
ones. The assert passes with `max |dW| = 0` and `max |dD| = 0`.

**So the 1966 rerun is a reproduction and cannot move a verdict.** It was run anyway, because a
reproduction that failed would mean the note's table did not come from this code.

## Sensitivity: the 2008 recomputed scale (NOT a correction to the Freeland-Hurst input)

Same wiring, same weights, `D` rebuilt from the Mathew-Luthey-Schulten values of

Hard code, beta = 0: gamma = 0, standard **42** vs shuffle median 56, fraction at or below
standard **0.0020**; gamma = 3, 48 vs 59, 0.0010; gamma = 10, 54 vs 60, 0.0050.

Three differences worth carrying:

- **The ordering survives the change of scale** at every cell. This is the robustness the earlier
  note could not claim.
- **The threshold drops**, to between beta = 1.0 and beta = 1.5, from between 1.75 and 2.0. So the
  threshold is a property of the polar-requirement scale, not of the code; quoting "beta ~ 2" as
  though it were a measured constant would be wrong.
- **The hard-code result weakens from exact to merely extreme**: on the 1966 scale none of 2000
  shuffles is as stable as the standard code; on the 2008 scale 2 to 10 in 2000 are. The
  strongest sentence the earlier note supports ("none of 2000 shuffles has as few") is
  scale-dependent and should not be written without naming the scale.
- **Which amino acid goes first changes**, Asp and Glu on the 1966 scale, Cys and Glu at beta = 1
  on the 2008 scale. The "Asp goes first" weirdness in the earlier note is thus partly an artifact
  of Asp being the 1966 scale's extreme, which supports reading it as a defect of the proxy rather
  than a finding about aspartate.
