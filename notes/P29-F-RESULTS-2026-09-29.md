   first claim and fall to about -0.41 by t = 12: that is the claims on exposure written in the
   first units, when her estimate, and so the rate, was still climbing from 0. The stall at 1.674
   costs only the slope after that: 0.011 over t in [12, 30] in stage 4. Diligence removes the
   slope (final fund -0.4163 against -0.4224) and cannot touch the level. So the plan's phrasing
   "the insurer underprices and its fund falls for good (-0.42 at t = 30)" mixes the two: -0.42 is
   mostly the start-up, the "for good" is the stall's -3e-3 per six units.
2. **One claim settles the question.** The records are noiseless and exactly proportional, so the
   least-squares fit equals beta from the first claim; what makes the rate move gradually
   (t = 6 to 10) is only the evidence share C/E, which the early high-smoke exposure dominates.
3. **Her stale estimate keeps a residual weight for good**, (E - C)/E = 1.7e-4, the last delay's
   exposure, so the rate ends 5.7e-5 below beta and the fund still drifts down by 4e-7 per six
   units. It shrinks like delay/t only once steady exposure outgrows the early exposure.
4. **Diligence lowers premium income**: the higher rate cleans the plant (c 0.943 to 0.952,
   final smoke 0.0566 to 0.0478), so the income rate falls 0.00268 to 0.00229 while the insurer
   now breaks even.
5. **Without her report diligence adds almost nothing** (harm 6.1337 to 6.1288): the court's fit
   and the insurer's claims fit see the same delayed records. The harm reduction from 6.13 to 0.80
   is all her early account; diligence corrects the price once claims arrive.

## Weirdness check (the agent's judgment, not evidence)

- **Reasonable for a real insurer:** pricing from an outside estimate until its own losses
  develop, then moving to its own experience, is how actuaries price new risks (an analogy to
  credibility and Bornhuetter-Ferguson reserving, uncited). Risk-based pricing lowering exposure
  and premium income together is reasonable. A court left wrong while the insurer is right is
  reasonable too: they learn separately.
- **Weird for a real insurer:** (a) one noiseless claim identifies the rate, so the "diligence"
  has nothing to learn; with noise the weight on its own fit should grow with evidence against a
  finite credibility for her report, and C/E ignores noise altogether. (b) Before any claim the
  insurer takes her estimate at full weight, with no skepticism of an outside number. (c) It knows
  the harm's form (smoke squared) and its exposure exactly. (d) It prices at beta going forward
  and never above it, so it never recoups the start-up loss; with unlimited credit it lives with
  a permanent -0.42 deficit. A real insurer loads for past losses, expenses and capital, or fails.
- **At the town's scale:** harm 2.5% lower and a plant 1 point cleaner are reasonable and small;
  the town's big gain is still her account.
