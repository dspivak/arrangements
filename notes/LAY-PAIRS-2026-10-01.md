## 4. Why the six went wrong

1. **My label, not the library: "eats" has no direction** (4, 12, and part of 1, 2). `hunt` is the cost
   `x y`, a meeting that costs both parties. In Lotka–Volterra the direction comes from the predator box,
   whose response is negative, so it climbs the cost the prey descends. I told the lay readers "the second
   eats the first", which the coupler alone does not say; with an ordinary part as the eater, the eater
   pays. Checked (piece 5): a *plain fox*, dying at the same rate alone but with an ordinary response,
   starves to 0 as the tank's eater, while the library's fox lives at 0.5.
2. **One part that only works with its partner: the predator box** (9). Its negative response turns every
   pull into a push. Told to keep in step with a chooser it dies faster (3e-12 against 3e-7 alone); the plain
   fox, pulled along, lives longer (0.016). The box is right with `hunt` and nowhere else. No library test
   or module wires it to anything else; the DC motor's opposite-signed cells use the same device, also only
   with their own coupler.
3. **Pairings with no meaning, which the ports cannot refuse** (10, 11, 1). A metronome's report is a
   phase, a point on a circle, but the port is `R^1`, so a phase can be watched toward a water level (the
   metronome stops), eaten, or used to bend a population as if a count were an angle.
4. **A meeting cost on things that can go negative** (1, 2). `x y` assumes both are amounts. A tank and a
   needle can go below zero, and then the meeting pays instead of costing, and the pair runs away (the
   rabbits overflow to NaN).

What the model got right and both readers missed: a part with no cost of its own gives way completely
(3: the needle comes all the way to the rabbits, which do not move); a band shares the difference by
stiffness, so the rest is at 1/3 and 2/3, not in the middle, and a frictionless weight's swing drains
into a dissipative partner (5); a watcher with its own drive rests offset from what it watches (11:
phase 2 = level 1 plus the drive).

## 5. Reasonable or weird, and what to worry about (the agent's judgment)

Reasonable at an ecological scale: 6 (a consumer on a renewing resource settles), 8 (a follower lags
what it follows), 5 (a coupled oscillator losing energy to a damped partner). Weird: an eater that starves
by eating (4, 12, my label), a population fleeing agreement (9), a clock stopped by watching a tank (11),
negative water (1). Nothing already in the book or the library rests on a weird case.

One consequence, **derived, not run**: if eating lives in each species' sign, a species that is eaten must
have the opposite sign from its eater, so a food web can be built from exact `hunt` couplers only if its
species split into two classes with every eating edge between them. A chain (grass, rabbit, fox) splits;
omnivory (a fox that eats both rabbits and the weasels that eat rabbits, a triangle) does not, and needs a
non-exact coupler that says who eats whom. Ch 1's populations sentence ("attached together pairwise by
who eats whom") puts the direction in the attachment, where the library does not.

## 6. What this does not establish

Twelve pairs of seven toy parts; one seed; the lay readers are language models told to be lay. It says
nothing about the §5.9 claims P26 exists to audit, whose composites are larger and built for their
purpose. It does show the audit's premise is short: P26 asks whether a conclusion survives an added
forcing term, and §4 item 2 is a part's *integrator* failing to mean the same thing under every forcing.

## 7. A food web where every species box is the same whatever it meets (predictions fixed before the run)

David, 2026-10-01: "I don't see what's wrong with a fox that eats weasels that eat rabbits … could it be
modeled in such a way that it wouldn't be dependent on knowing what we were going to do ahead of time …
That's what really makes something compositional." The construction: every species is the same ordinary
box (log population, positive response, its own growth or death); the direction lives in the coupler, a
**directed eats** `<0|2> -> I` with the non-exact forcing `-beta y dx + delta x dy` on (prey, eater), so
the prey loses in proportion to the eater and the eater gains in proportion to the prey.

Predictions: (P1) rabbits + foxes on a directed eats gives Lotka–Volterra step for step, equal to the
library's composite (`lotka_volterra`, signed sharps and `hunt`) to round-off. (P2) No assignment of signs
to the three species with exact `x y` or `-x y` couplers makes all three eating edges of the triangle point
the right way (checked by enumeration). (P3) The triangle (fox eats rabbit and weasel, weasel eats rabbit)
on directed eats equals the hand-written three-species equations to round-off, and with logistic rabbits
the three settle or cycle with all three alive at some parameters. (P4) The same ordinary fox box, wired to
a band or "in step" instead, is pulled along, not pushed away (already seen: 0.016 against 3e-12).

**Results** (`arrangements/food_web.py`, `scripts/food_web_run.py`, `notes/lay-pairs/food_web.{json,log}`).
(P1) **Held exactly**: ordinary rabbits and foxes on a directed eats agree with the library's signed-sharp
Lotka–Volterra to 0.0 over 2000 steps while the populations swing by 4.6 and 3.2 (a control with delta
moved by 0.1% differs at step 500). (P2) **Held**: of the 64 sign assignments with exact couplers, 2 make the
chain rabbit, weasel, fox point the right way and 0 make the triangle. (P3) **Held**: the triangle equals
the hand-written equations to 1e-6 (float32) over 20000 steps, and settles at the designed rest
(0.500, 0.200, 0.100), all three alive, lowest point 0.086. (P4) held earlier: the ordinary fox is pulled
along by "in step".

So the omnivory triangle *can* be modeled, and in a way that is compositional in David's sense: one
species box serves every web, and adding an eating edge is adding a coupler, with nothing in any species
changed. The price: the directed coupler is not exact (`d omega = (beta + delta) dx ^ dy`), so the web has
no single cost it descends; Lotka–Volterra already had none in its ordinary chart (its conserved quantity is
not a potential), and the library's exact version bought one only by the sign trick.
