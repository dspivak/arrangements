### `tests/test_tilted_cue.py`

````
A weak cue over a low ridge releases a large potential: the tilted double well.

**Question.** The book's Ch 1 (``sec.check_claims``) says of cues and words that "as pushes
they are very weak", yet a few words organize a lot of activity. The model cited for it is the
attention paper's capture example, ``tests/test_cue.py``, whose preference cost
``phi_lambda(g) = (lambda/4) g^2 (1 - g)^2`` has two *equal* wells. There the energy goes the
other way: the paper's cue does work ``W = 11.6`` on the context and the composite releases
``0.38`` after it ends (the domain check of 2026-09-29, Group E2 entry 3). With equal wells it
cannot go the other way: the composite starts and ends at ``V = 0``, so what it releases after
the cue is at most what the cue put in. Is a cue that is weak *in energy*, next to what follows
it, in the model at all?

**Model.** The composite of ``test_cue.py`` unchanged -- the rendezvous parent with the context
coordinate ``g``, two subsidiaries, composed by ``compose_seq`` in the library, cost the sum of
the constituents' -- except the preference cost, which is tilted by an exact change:

    phi'(g) = A g (g - c)(g - 1),   phi(g) = A (g^4/4 - (1 + c) g^3/3 + c g^2/2).

The wells stay at ``g = 0`` (meet) and ``g = 1`` (keep the separation, the resting state) and the
ridge is at ``c``; ``phi(1) - phi(0) = A (2c - 1)/12 = D`` and
``phi(c) - phi(1) = A (1 - c)^3 (1 + c)/12 = eps``, so ``(A, c)`` is solved from ``(D, eps)``. The
resting state sits in a *shallow* well, ``eps`` below a ridge, beside a well ``D`` deeper. Since
the coupling ``K`` can be zero at every ``g``, the lowest ridge of the whole ``V`` between the two
rests is exactly ``eps`` above the resting state. The cue is the paper's shape,
``u(t) = a b_0(t/T)``; its only effect on the state is the force ``+u`` on ``g`` (checked
below), so its work is ``W = sum_k u_k (g_{k+1} - g_k)``, and what the composite releases after
the cue is ``V(T) - V(end)``. Step ``h = 0.001`` (the numbers agree to four digits at ``h = 0.002``
and ``0.0005``); all five mobilities equal ``h``, as in ``test_cue.py``.

**Prediction** (written before any run, `notes/ch1-tests-2026-09-29/tilted-cue.md` in the book
repo): the cue's work scales with the barrier ``eps``, the release with ``D``, so
``released / W`` grows like ``D / eps``; the cue only lifts the state over the ridge, and the fall
is paid by the composite's own potential.

**Result.** For the *minimal* cue -- the amplitude bisected to where capture just happens -- of
duration ``T = 0.2``, the prediction holds in direction, with two corrections. The release after
the cue is ``D + eps`` plus a small coupling excess (``4.27, 16.26, 64.25`` for ``D = 4, 16, 64`` at
``eps = 0.25``); ``W`` is between ``1.36 eps`` and ``3.0 eps`` and never below ``eps``; the ratio is
``5.7, 35, 188``. (i) At fixed ``D = 16``, ``W`` grows like ``eps^0.8`` rather than ``eps``
(``0.160, 0.464, 1.445`` for ``eps = 1/16, 1/4, 1``): a brief push dissipates, and the ridge's
distance from the resting state shrinks with ``eps``. (ii) At fixed ``eps``, ``W`` *falls* as
``D`` grows (``0.744, 0.464, 0.341``), since the ridge moves nearer the current well; so the ratio
grows faster than ``D / eps``. The briefer the cue, the more work it takes: at ``D = 16``,
``eps = 0.25`` the work done by the time ``g`` crosses the ridge is ``1.11, 0.46, 0.34, 0.30`` for
``T = 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1``, falling toward ``eps = 0.25``, the energy bound. The control: the
paper's untilted example gives ``W = 11.63``, released ``0.385``; its minimal ``T = 0.2`` cue
gives ``W = 2.88``, released ``1.09``: released below ``W``, as it must be.

**But that ratio is a knife-edge** (found after the prediction, when a first version of the
last test failed). The release *after* the cue is large only when the cue ends before the state
falls, and for the paper's fixed bump that holds within about ``1%`` of the minimal amplitude: at
``D = 64``, ``T = 0.2``, ``1.01`` times the minimal cue crosses the ridge while still on, keeps
pushing along the steep fall, does ``W = 1.30`` and leaves ``0.44`` after it. What is robust:
the cue's work up to the ridge crossing, between ``eps`` and ``5 eps`` for every cue tried
(``T >= 0.05``, up to ``1.5`` times minimal); the composite's own net release
``V(0) - V(end) = D`` for every capturing cue; and so the ratio of that release to *all* the
cue's work, ``D / W = 1.1, 3.5, 11`` for ``D = 4, 16, 64`` at ``1.5`` times minimal, which grows
with ``D``, sub-linearly, and exceeds ``1`` only once ``D`` is well past the few units a
fixed-shape cue spends riding the fall.

**What it does not show.** (1) A cue that stopped at the crossing would do only the work to the
ridge; the paper's bump cannot stop there, and nothing here models a cue that responds to where
the reader is. (2) The tilt, like the paper's wells, is supplied by hand: nothing here says how a
reader comes to be poised, one ``eps`` from a well ``D`` deeper. (3) Released potential is
dissipated by the composite's own descent; it is energy, not "activity" in the book's wider
sense, and nothing here measures how much the parts do beyond the positions moving. (4) The
discrete run is checked, not proved: every number here is of the explicit Euler map at the
stated step.
````

### `tests/test_dispersal_momentum.py`

````
Dispersal with momentum: does a push's energy spread irrecoverably through a collective?

The second half of the book plan's P17 (AGENTDOCPLAN.md §8.2 in the book repository), in
David's words (2026-09-26): "in a ratchet, i want the click to be the sound of energy
spreading irrecoverably through the handle of the ratchet", and "if it remained right
there, the spring would bounce back up and the ratchet would go backwards".
``tests/test_dispersal.py`` ran the same collective under descent, where a displacement
carries no energy. Here the parent's integrator is the phase coalgebra (and its damped
variant): each placement carries a momentum, and a part is *pushed* by starting it with
momentum ``xi_0`` outward at a common equilibrium. Nothing else changes: frozen parts
sending ``(1, 1)``, the parent's cost ``(1/2)(nu - sum_j kappa_j)^2`` on the total it sends.

**The predictions, fixed in advance and written here before any construction code for this
file existed. Each must be able to fail.**

Linear analysis first. Near a common equilibrium the cost is ``(1/2) g^2 (sum_j x_j)^2``
with ``g = kappa'(p*)``: its Hessian has rank one. The one direction it sees, the total,
is a spring; the other ``n - 1`` directions are free. A push ``xi_0`` on part 0 splits into
``xi_0 / n`` on every part along the spring and a free remainder, ``xi_0 (1 - 1/n)`` on
part 0 and ``-xi_0 / n`` on each other part; the free remainder carries the fraction
``1 - 1/n`` of the push's kinetic energy.

(5) *Undamped, a small push: the spring's share comes back and the rest never does.* Over
many oscillations each part's momentum averages to its free share: ``xi_0 (1 - 1/n)`` for
the pushed part, ``-xi_0 / n`` for each other, within 5%; for ``n = 1`` it averages to zero.
So the fraction ``1 - 1/n`` of the energy put in spreads into motions the cost cannot see
and does not return to the spring. Fails if the averages differ from the free shares.

(6) *Damped, a small push lands the configuration somewhere new.* With the damped phase
integrator (a fraction ``c`` of momentum removed per step), the spring's share rings down
and returns, and the free share coasts a distance ``mu xi_0 / c`` before it stops: the pushed
part ends displaced by ``(mu xi_0 / c)(1 - 1/n)`` and each other by ``-(mu xi_0 / c) / n``,
within 5%, with the onward message back at ``nu``; for ``n = 1`` it ends where it started.
This is the click in this model: the push's energy is gone, dissipated, and the
configuration keeps the part of the push the cost could not see.

(7) *Undamped, redundancy decides whether a small push loses a part.* At target
``nu = 1.5`` and push ``xi_0 = 0.05`` (kinetic energy ``~6e-4``): with ``n = 3`` (the other
two can cover 1.5) the pushed part coasts out of reach, past distance 5 within 5000 steps,
and does not come back; with ``n = 2`` (the other can send at most 1) it moves along the
one-dimensional family of minima until the other part reaches the kernel's peak, turns, and
never gets past distance 1.2 in 100,000 steps. This goes beyond the linear analysis, which
says only that the free share coasts; whether the others compensate fast enough for the
pushed part to leave, and whether the ``n = 2`` part is turned back rather than escaping, are
the nonlinear run's. Fails if the ``n = 3`` part is turned back or the ``n = 2`` part escapes.

````

### `tests/test_scattering.py`

````
Scattering: random kicks on a collective's placements.

The book plan's P6 stage (c) (AGENTDOCPLAN.md §8.2 in the book repository; David,
2026-09-25, "if it can, then maybe we should include that test too?"), whose claim, Claude's
and *proposed*, was: "with the supply cut, a bounded finite random kick carries parts out of
kernel reach, where kernel and gradient both vanish so nothing returns them." The collective
is ``tests/test_dispersal.py``'s (frozen parts sending ``(1, 1)`` on a spatial parent with
the total cost), and the parent's integrator is ``rand.kicked_configuration``: the descent
step, then an independent kick of ``+a`` or ``-a`` on every placement, each with probability
1/2. It is paired with a pseudo-random sampler by ``rand.seed``; many runs are one ``vmap``
over seeds. A part is *in reach* while ``kappa > 0.05``, i.e. ``|p| < r = 2.19``.

**The predictions, fixed in advance and written here before any construction code for this
file existed. Each must be able to fail.**

(8) *With the need present, noise does what a push does: redundancy decides.* Target 1.5,
kick ``a = 0.05``, 50 runs of 20,000 steps. With ``n = 3`` (any two can cover the target)
the kicks drive a random walk along the family of minima, which is unbounded in the
direction of losing a part, and at least 80% of runs end with a part out of reach. With
``n = 2`` (the other can send at most 1) the family is bounded, and no part in any run ever
passes ``|p| = 1.5``. Fails if the ``n = 3`` collective keeps its parts or the ``n = 2``
one loses one.

(9) *With nothing holding them, scattered parts do come back -- ever more rarely.* This
corrects the plan's "nothing returns them". With the parent's cost removed (the supply cut),
each placement is a simple random walk: no gradient returns a part, but a walk on the line
is recurrent and returns by itself. The probability that a part is in reach at step ``t`` is
exactly the binomial ``P(|p_0 + a (2B - t)| < r)``, ``B ~ Bin(t, 1/2)``; the fraction of
runs with the part in reach matches it within three standard errors at ``t = 100, 1000,
10000``, and between ``t = 1000`` and ``10000`` it falls by ``sqrt(10) ~ 3.16`` within 15%.
Fails if the frequencies miss the binomial or the decay is not ``t^{-1/2}``. On a plane the
walk is still recurrent, only more slowly; in three or more dimensions it is transient and a
scattered part would, with positive probability, never return. The collective module places
parts on a line, so that case is not run.

**What happened** (2026-09-26, recorded after the run, the predictions above untouched).
(8) held, narrowly: 82% of ``n = 3`` runs lost a part (threshold 80%); the farthest any
``n = 2`` part got was 1.319. (9) held in its first half -- the frequencies match the
binomial at every ``t`` -- and was **refuted in its second as stated**: from ``t = 1000`` to
``10000`` the fraction in reach fell by 2.145, not 3.16. The error was the window, not the
law: at ``t = 1000`` the walk's spread ``a sqrt(t) = 1.6`` is still inside the reach
``r = 2.19``, so the ``t^{-1/2}`` tail has not set in; the exact binomial itself falls by 2.24
there, by 3.02 from ``10^4`` to ``10^5``, and by 3.15 from ``10^5`` to ``10^6``. The test below
asserts the corrected claim, added after the result: the runs match the binomial through
``t = 10^5``, and between ``10^4`` and ``10^5``, where ``a sqrt(t) >> r``, they fall by
``sqrt(10)`` within 15%.
````

### `tests/test_dispersal.py`

````
Dispersal: can a collective's parts be driven out of the relations, and do they come back?

The book plan's P17 (AGENTDOCPLAN.md §8.2 in the book repository), David, 2026-09-26: "in
a ratchet, i want the click to be the sound of energy spreading irrecoverably through the
handle of the ratchet. is that testable? if so would it handle the destruction idea?", and
§1.4's "the destruction of a building is just a dis-integration": no matter disappears, it
stops being in the relations. Here a part is in the relations while the parent's kernel
reaches it, and the collective is ``arrangements.collective``: ``n`` frozen contributors,
each sending ``(SIG, WGT)``, on a spatial parent whose cost is on the total it sends onward,
``(1/2)(nu - h~)^2`` with ``h~ = SIG sum_j kappa_j``, and whose only mobile coordinates are
the placements. A part is displaced by starting it displaced; nothing else is done to it.

**The predictions, fixed in advance and written here before the collective module or any
construction existed. Each must be able to fail.**

(1) *The collective's message returns.* After one part is displaced, small or far, the
onward signal returns to ``nu`` whenever the other parts can supply it. Fails if descent
stalls short of ``nu`` with the others able to supply it.

(2) *Its configuration does not, and what stays out is shared evenly.* With all parts at one
equilibrium distance, a small outward displacement ``delta`` of one part is recovered by
that part only in the fraction ``1/n``; each other part moves ``delta/n`` inward, and nothing
further happens however long the run. This is a theorem in the linear regime, not a
discovery: the cost sees only the total, its minima form an ``(n-1)``-dimensional family,
and descent from a displaced point moves along the single direction the cost sees,
``(kappa'_1, ..., kappa'_n)``, which is uniform here, so it removes the displacement's
projection on that direction, ``delta/n`` in each coordinate, and never touches the rest.
What the run checks is that the nonlinear kernel and the discrete step keep it (fraction
within 5% of ``1/n`` at ``delta = 0.05``), and it fails if they do not. It refutes the plan's
earlier guess that return *time* grows with the number of parts: for ``n >= 2`` the
configuration never returns at all, and for ``n = 1`` it returns completely. So the
irreversibility here is not slowness; it is displacement in directions the cost cannot see.

(3) *Whether a far part is called back is decided by whether the rest can cover it.* With
``nu = 1.5`` and a part displaced to ``d = 3`` (kernel ``~ 0.004``): for ``n = 2`` the other
part alone sends at most ``SIG = 1 < nu``, a residual persists, and the far part is pulled
all the way back; for ``n = 3`` the other two can send ``1.5``, the residual vanishes, and
the far part stays out, within 10% of ``d``, for ``10^5`` steps. Fails if the ``n = 3`` part
returns or the ``n = 2`` part does not.

(4) *Total dispersal comes back, at a rate set by distance and not by the number of parts.*
All ``n`` parts displaced together to ``d``: the onward signal falls to ``n kappa(d)``, near
zero, and returns to within 1% of ``nu``. Far out ``kappa`` is small and each part moves as a
lone part would, ``dp/dt = -(mu nu / tau) p kappa(p)``, so the return time is the quadrature
``(tau / mu nu) int e^{p^2 / 2 tau} / p dp``, which grows like ``e^{d^2 / 2 tau}``. The
measured return time agrees with that quadrature within 15% at the largest ``d``, and the
times for ``n = 1, 2, 4`` agree with each other within 15% there. Fails if the time depends
on ``n`` at large ``d``, or does not track the quadrature.

If (2)-(4) hold, the answer to P17 in this model is: a part started far from a collective that
can do without it stays out for good, deterministically, and the configuration keeps the
displacement the cost cannot see; a collective started far out as a whole comes back as long
as the need persists, at a time exponential in distance squared. Nothing here drives a part
out: every run starts from the displaced state (``test_dispersal_momentum.py`` is the run in
which a pushed part coasts out by the dynamics). Irrecoverable dispersal needs
redundancy, not noise.
````

### `tests/test_cascade.py`

````
The cascade: a fund that pays its parts, a shock, and how deep the damage runs.

The book plan's P19 (a) (AGENTDOCPLAN.md §8.2 in the book repository), from David's reading of
ruin (2026-09-26): "it's an account *connected to disintegration* that falls below 0. as the
shockwave hits the parts, they come apart. their own integrity forces them to leave the
collective", and, of the depth, "let's try several depths and see if your thing works". The
pieces are ``arrangements/cascade.py`` on ``arrangements/collective.py``:

* a **worker** holds an amplitude ``a`` (its held form) on the supply it receives, with the
  cost ``(b/2) a^2 - (alpha a + beta) h_1`` on the supply channel ``h_1`` of its input, so its
  amplitude settles at ``alpha h_1 / b`` and its placement moves toward whatever supplies it
  (``beta`` is a standing want, so an unsupplied part still looks for supply);
* a **source** is a fixed part off to the side, supplying ``s`` on the same channel: the
  elsewhere a worker can go;
* a **fund layer** turns a collective into a unit ``I -> B``: its balance ``f`` pays every
  part in reach the wage ``g(f)`` through a smooth gate, is paid what arrives on the unit's own
  supply channel, and pays a fixed overhead ``c_0``; it passes the collective's product up;
* at the top, a **market** pays ``r`` times the product (one level), or a fixed supply
  arrives (the nested runs).

A *shock* or *drain* below is the fund's balance reduced by ``S`` by hand, the run from there
(``shocker``, ``depth_runner``); the top supply is an exterior input (``supplied``), so its cut
is the environment's schedule; neither is an event in a run (STATUS.md §2, "Not evidence of an
event").

**Three choices, said as choices, because they decide what can happen.** (i) Supply and
product travel on two channels (``dim H = 2``), or the workers would supply each other. (ii)
The fund pays a fixed overhead: without one, income and wages both scale with the wage and
nothing can tip, so the overhead is what makes a threshold possible at all. (iii) The fund's
flow is not a gradient; it is a record, one-way (``tests/test_insurance.py``).

**The predictions, fixed in advance and written here before ``cascade.py`` or any
construction code existed. Each must be able to fail.**

One level (P19 (a); its three predictions, sharpened, not softened):

(10) *A threshold, all or none.* There is a shock size below which the fund recovers and
every worker stays in reach, and above which the fund collapses and the workers leave for the
source. The existence of a threshold is built in by the overhead and the revenue feedback (the
fund's balance has an unstable fixed point); what can fail is the rest: that the workers
actually leave rather than fade in place, and that the number who leave jumps from none to all
within a band of shock sizes 10% wide. Fails if the response is graded.

(11) *Nowhere to go.* With the source removed, the same above-threshold shock disperses
nothing: the workers' amplitudes fall to near zero and their placements move less than 0.1.
Fails if they disperse with no elsewhere to go.

(12) *Redundancy buys margin?* P19's prediction: at fixed total output the threshold rises
with the number of workers. The agent's counter-guess, fixed here too: with identical wiring
the fund sees only totals, so the threshold is the same for ``n = 2, 4, 8`` within 10%, and
P19's (3) fails. One of the two must fail.

Nested (depth ``D = 1..4``, each unit a collective of two sub-units and a fund, workers and
their sources at the bottom, a fixed supply into the top fund):

(13) *A permanent loss runs to every depth.* Cut the top supply below the top's overhead and
the workers leave at every depth tested. Fails if some depth holds.

(14) *A transient shock is filtered by the funds between.* Drain the top fund once and let it
recover. Each fund below is a first-order filter on the wage it passes down. With **fast**
funds the dip reaches the bottom nearly whole (the bottom's lowest wage within 10% of the
top's at every depth) and the workers leave at every depth; with **slow** funds (reserves) the
bottom's lowest wage rises with every level added, and from some depth ``D <= 4`` on no worker
leaves. So a shock the top survives runs only to a limited depth where the levels hold
reserves, and to any depth where they do not. Fails if slow funds do not stop it, or fast
funds do.

**What happened** (2026-09-26, recorded after the runs; the predictions above untouched).

*Two things found while building, before any prediction was tested.* A fund in surplus grew
without bound, so a ``leak`` (the fraction of its balance it spends) was added to give it an
equilibrium to shock. And exact composition made the fund *not* a record: the wage depends on
the balance, so the workers' want is pulled back through the wage wire onto the balance, at
``g'(f)(alpha sum kappa a + beta sum kappa)`` -- the needy prop up the purse (retroactivity).
The runs below use the one-way wire (``record``), which cancels exactly that term; the exact
wire is kept as its own test, and it raises the threshold from 20.22 to 21.78. A placement
mobility of 0.5 was also found unstable at two workers (a 2-cycle) and lowered to 0.1.

(10) Held, with a caveat: at balance 19.74 the threshold is 20.22; below it every worker
stays, above it the fund falls to -18.4 and all four go to the source, where they hold their
form (amplitude 1.0). But all-or-none is forced by symmetry here -- a worker's leaving depends
only on the wage against the outside offer, the same for every worker -- so it was not tested.
(11) Held: with no source the threshold is 18.05 and above it the workers stay where they were
and fade to amplitude 0. (12) P19's rise holds in letter (19.12, 20.22, 21.86 at n = 2, 4, 8)
and the agent's flat counter-guess fails; but the rise is the balance's (18.80, 19.74, 21.53,
from the source-supplied product of more workers), the margin stays flat (0.32, 0.48, 0.33),
and without a source the threshold is 18.045 at every n. Neither prediction's mechanism, so
redundancy buys no margin here.
(13) Held: a permanent cut to 30% of the top supply sends the workers to their sources at
every depth, fast funds or slow. (14) Held for a shock that stops the top's wage (four times
its reserve): through fast funds the bottom's lowest wage is 0.025 of equilibrium at depth 4
and the workers leave at every depth; through slow funds the first level below the top absorbs
it (0.78) and no worker leaves from depth 2 on, the bottom's lowest wage rising 0.78, 0.92, 0.95.
**Refuted in part:** a moderate shock (95% of the top's reserve) is attenuated level by level
even through fast funds (0.26, 0.39, 0.48, 0.55 at depth 4) and moves no worker anywhere. How
deep a shock runs depends on how long it stops the wage against how fast the funds between
respond, not on speed alone.
````

````
The ratchet: shock, disperse, re-form, shock again -- does re-forming remember?

The book plan's P21 (AGENTDOCPLAN.md §8.2 in the book repository), from David, 2026-09-26:
"shocks can break things apart but then they can come back together if the pieces sustain and
if they attract again ... or often they'll reform in a different way". Everything here runs on
``arrangements/cascade.py`` as it stands (the collective of §3.13, the fund layer of §3.14);
this file adds no mechanism to it.

**What re-forming is, settled with David before the run.** After the collapse of the cascade
run (§5.9 item 19) the fund is dead and stays dead: with no worker in reach it earns 0.16
against an overhead of 2 and sits at -18.4 forever, paying no wage; a worker is drawn to
supply, so nothing draws the parts back on its own. The pieces do sustain -- they hold
amplitude 1.0 at the source. So re-forming comes from the experimenter, by (a) a sum added to
the fund's balance by hand (``add_f``), with (b) the source box's offer set lower for a window
of steps and set back (``window``) as the second route; a "shock" is the balance reduced by
hand (``collapsed``). Every phase is one run from the previous run's end state with one such
change; none of these words names an event in a run (STATUS.md §2, "Not evidence of an
event"). Parts attracting
*each other* is not this model: supply and product ride separate channels precisely so that
workers do not feed one another.

**The landscape, derived by hand before the run.** A worker's placement ``p`` feels only its
own cost ``(b/2)a^2 - (alpha a + beta) h_1``, so it ascends the supply it receives,

    h_1(p) = g kappa(p) + s kappa(p - d),     kappa(x) = exp(-x^2 / 2 tau),

with ``g = g(f)`` the fund's wage at the origin and ``s`` the source's offer at distance ``d``.
The amplitude ``a`` enters only as a positive rate ``(alpha a + beta)``, so the *stationary
placements are exactly the stationary points of* ``h_1``, independent of ``a`` and of the other
workers (they send on the product channel, which the cost does not read). Writing
``rho = g / s``, a stationary point solves ``rho = phi(p)`` with

    phi(p) = ((d - p) / p) exp(d (2p - d) / 2 tau).

``log phi`` turns at ``p (d - p) = tau``, whose roots ``p_1 < p_2`` (``p_1 + p_2 = d``,
``p_1 p_2 = tau``) give a local minimum and a local maximum of ``phi``:

    phi_min = (p_2/p_1) exp(-d (p_2 - p_1) / 2 tau),  phi_max = (p_1/p_2) exp(d (p_2 - p_1) / 2 tau).

For ``rho`` between them the landscape is bistable (home, ridge, source); above ``phi_max`` only
home survives; below ``phi_min`` only the source does. At ``d = 2.5``, ``tau = 0.8``:
``p_1 = 0.376788``, ``p_2 = 2.123212``, ``phi_min = 0.36790``, ``phi_max = 2.71797``, and their
ratio is ``7.3877``. At ``d = 2.0``, ``tau = 0.5``: ``0.34453``, ``2.90277``, ratio ``8.4254``.

**The predictions, fixed here before any construction code in this file existed. Each must be
able to fail.** The quantities above are hand-derived and quasi-static (they hold ``g`` fixed
while ``p`` moves); the runs are the coupled system, where the balance moves too.

(R1) *Money cannot outbid the elsewhere.* With the wage capped at the outside offer
(``wmax = s = 1``), no injection into the collapsed fund re-forms anything: at injections of
10, 100, 1000 and 10000, after 10^5 steps every placement is still past 2.0. Because
``g(f) < wmax = s`` for every balance, so ``rho < 1 < phi_max`` always. Fails if any worker
returns at any injection.

(R2) *One ratio decides it, and the geometry fixes the ratio.* With the fund's balance clamped
(mobility 0, so the wage is a dial), the wage at which a worker at home leaves is
``g_out = phi_min s`` and the wage at which a worker at the source returns is
``g_in = phi_max s``, each within 2% of the closed form, in both geometries above. Their ratio
is the hysteresis: **the wage that brings them back is 7.39 times the wage at which they
left**, within 2%. Fails if either critical wage misses by more than 2%, or if there is no
bistable band.

(R3) *Parts do not call each other back.* The two critical wages are the same for ``n = 1`` and
``n = 4`` workers, to 1%: a part at the source is held there by the landscape alone, and the
others' presence at home does not pull it in. Fails if the critical wages move with ``n`` --
which they would if the parts attracted one another.

(R4) *The loop closes when the fund can outbid the elsewhere.* At ``wmax = 4 s`` the whole cycle
runs: the collective equilibrates, a drain above the threshold ``S_c`` collapses it and sends
every worker to the source, and an injection above ``R_c`` brings them all home again. The
critical injection is set by the landscape: ``R_c`` puts the balance at ``f_in`` with
``g(f_in) = phi_max s``, within 20% (the balance falls while they walk home, so the run may need
more, not less). Fails if no injection re-forms it, or if ``R_c`` misses by more than 20%.

(R5) *A fixed point forgets.* When every worker returns, the re-formed collective is the
original: each placement, each amplitude and the balance agree with the first equilibrium to
1e-4, and the second break threshold equals the first to 0.5%. So re-forming by itself leaves
no memory. Fails if the re-formed collective's threshold differs by more than 0.5% with the
same membership.

(R6) *What re-forming can carry is membership, and it is a scar, not a ratchet.* Give the
workers different placement mobilities (0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.025) so that they walk home at
different speeds, and re-form them through a window that closes on its own -- the injected
balance decays while they walk, and the wage falls back below ``g_in`` -- or, in the second
route, through a window of ``W`` steps during which the source's offer drops. Then some come
home and some do not. Prediction: (i) there is a band of injections (and a band of ``W``) at
which the collective re-forms *short a part*; (ii) the re-formed collective is weaker, not
stronger: its threshold is more than 1% *below* the original's, and the loss tracks the
balance it can hold, not the margin above it (the cascade run's (12): the balance rises with
the number of workers, the margin does not). Fails if the band is empty -- all or none even at
different speeds -- or if a collective re-formed short a part withstands a *larger* shock,
which would be a ratchet.

(R7) *Both routes are the same condition.* The collective re-forms through the second route --
the fund refilled and the source's offer dropped to ``s_low`` for a while -- exactly when
``g / s_low > phi_max``, with the critical ``s_low`` within 5% of ``g / phi_max`` for the wage
the refilled fund pays. Fails if the critical offer misses by more than 5%, or if the two
routes need different ratios.

**What happened** (2026-09-26, written after the runs; the predictions above untouched).

The whole file takes about thirteen minutes: the walks are slow near a fold, and every number
here is a transition found by repeated runs.

*Two things found while building, before any prediction was tested.* (i) At a wage cap of 4 the

guarded by ``one_step_move``, the largest coordinate change in one further step, which is below 1e-9
at every state below. (ii) An injection into a fund with nobody in reach **drains away**:
after 10^5 steps the balance is back at -18.391 whether 10 or 10000 was paid in, because the
overhead and the leak spend it and no product arrives.

(R1) **Held.** At injections of 10, 100, 1000 and 10000 no placement moves off the source

(R2) **Held**, to 0.03% rather than the 2% allowed: ``g_out = 0.36800`` against

(R3) **Held** exactly: the same two critical wages for ``n = 1`` and ``n = 4``, to every digit

(R4) **Held in the loop, refuted in the number.** At ``wmax = 4`` the cycle runs: equilibrium at

(R5) **Held, exactly.** When all four return the re-formed collective *is* the original: balance

channels, so every part is supplied by the fund and by the elsewhere and never by another part.
The parts depend on one another only through the paymaster, one hop -- a part that leaves lowers
the balance and so everyone's wage, which is what makes the scar -- so everything below is about a
collective **held together by what it is paid**. The same cycle on a collective whose parts supply
each other is the book plan's P21 stage (b).

break. The one thing it can carry is **who did not come back**, and even that sticks only where the
door closes behind
them -- where what re-formed cannot pay enough to call the rest home. What a break leaves is a
scar and never a ratchet: the same shock breaks the re-formed collective sooner. And the part left
out is not gone: it still sends its product in from the source, which is why the three that came
````

### `tests/test_carving.py`

````
A form played long enough carves a rest that was not there.

The claim is David's (2026-09-28: "i think a new form can start to carve out a new capacity. is
that not something we've imagined yet? this is how memory works, and how river beds work"), and
this file is its first run. The predictions were fixed before any of this code existed, in
``notes/CARVING-PREDICTIONS-2026-09-28.md``; the book's plan holds them as open-mathematics item 8a.
**Every docstring below says whether its test could have come out otherwise**; the ones that could
not are closed-form checks of the machinery, and only the others are results.

**The run is composed** (plan P43 (b) job 3, Claude Opus 5.5, de71554a, 2026-10-10): every result below
is ``Phi`` of ``carving.reader``, a quick part (state ``q``, cost ``q^4/4 - u q``, hearing the note), a
slow part, the tuning (state ``w``, cost ``gamma (w - w0)^2/2 + beta (w - w0)^4/4``), and a stateless
coupling between them with cost ``-w q^2/2``, wired, with the note on the reader's port. Until then the
reader was one box holding both coordinates under one cost (P43 (a)); that box and the hand loop
``by_hand`` are kept beside the composite and checked equal to it, at the runs' settings. The
non-reciprocal arm is a coupling with a 1-form and no cost (``carving.nonreciprocal_coupling``).

The one-box reader was one component ``I -> <R^2 | R^2>`` whose state is a fast coordinate ``q`` and a slow
one ``w``, its response the diagonal sharp ``diag(eta_q, eta_w)`` with ``eta_w << eta_q``. The cost,
with the note ``u`` arriving on the exterior input, is

    k(q, w; u) = q^4/4 - w q^2/2 - u q + gamma (w - w0)^2 / 2 + beta (w - w0)^4 / 4,

so ``w`` is the curvature of the fast landscape at the origin: for ``w < 0`` the reader has one rest,
``q = 0``; for ``w > 0`` it has two, ``q = +- sqrt(w)``. Both coordinates descend this one cost, so
the slow rule is ``w' = w + eta_w (q^2/2 - gamma (w - w0) - beta (w - w0)^3)``: activity deepens,
disuse decays. The reader starts at ``w0 = -1/2``, one rest and no memory.

**A finding from the construction, before any prediction was tested.** The first cost had no quartic
term in ``w``, only ``gamma (w - w0)^2 / 2``. With a quadratic deepening against a linear decay there
is no stable memory at all: for ``gamma > 1/2`` the only rest is the blank one, and for
``gamma < 1/2`` the carved rest exists but is unstable, since on the slaved branch ``q^2 = w`` the
slow flow has ``d/dw [q^2/2 - gamma(w - w0)] = 1/2 - gamma > 0``, and ``w`` ran away to overflow. So a
carved memory needs the deepening to saturate; the quartic is the least that does it. With
``beta (w - w0)^4 / 4`` added, the slaved slow flow ``g(w) = -w/2 + gamma(w - w0) + beta(w - w0)^3``
has a stable carved rest at ``w* = 1/2``, ``q* = +- sqrt(1/2)``, whenever ``gamma + beta = 1/4`` and
``gamma + 3 beta > 1/2``; this file takes ``beta = 1/5``, ``gamma = 1/20``. The blank rest
``(0, w0)`` stays stable, and an unstable rest near ``w = 0.22`` separates the two basins.

Results, against the five predictions (Claude Opus 5 (1M), session 1a70010f, 2026-09-28):

1. **Held.** The hold threshold is 1337 steps and is sharp to one: a note held 1336 steps leaves the
   reader at the blank rest, 1337 leaves it at the carved one, ``(0.7051, 0.4972)`` against the
   predicted ``(sqrt(1/2), 1/2)``, and it stays. **Unpredicted, and a result:** below the threshold
   the tuning is not left untouched but holding a *fading* trace — ``w = -0.402`` where it started at
   ``-0.5`` — which goes back on the tuning's own decay time, ``1/(eta_w gamma) = 10^4`` steps. So a
   note too short to carve still leaves a mark that has to wear off, and what the threshold decides
   is whether the mark holds itself up.
2. **Held.** The fast landscape's curvature at ``q = 0`` flips sign, so the reader ends with a rest
   where it had none, and a kick that does not cross ``q = 0`` decays back into it.
3. **Closed form, as predicted.** The carved rest is a joint fixed point at ``u = 0`` and was one
   before the note played. Carving is basin selection at the joint level; what is new is the fast
   landscape at the slow coordinate's new value. The book must say it that way.
4. **Held.** The joint cost never rises along a run, with the note on or off, so dissipation stays
   non-negative and the accounting is unharmed by the tuning moving.
5. **Held, and it is the warning.** A tuning driven by ``q`` rather than ``q^2 / 2`` is non-reciprocal
   and no longer descends the cost: the joint cost rises on 2915 of 8000 steps, by as much as 1.0e-4
   (recorded as 0.80 until P43 (b) job 3, which found the figure matched no run),
   and the reader ends at ``(1.10, 1.22)``, at no rest of the exact model. So a carving tuning is
   accountable only while it descends the same cost its fast coordinate does.

6. **Held.** With the note arriving as rain rather than held once — on for a percentage of each
   period — what decides the outcome is the percentage, not the elapsed time: 9% carves and 8% does
   not, at periods 100 and 200 alike. (David, 2026-09-28, asked for this: "should have been something
   like a weekly rainfall … if you get to a certain amount, then something happens.")
7. **Refuted in both halves, and the refutation is the better statement.** I predicted bursts would
   beat drizzle at short periods, the deepening being convex, and drizzle would beat bursts at long
   ones, the tuning decaying between. At a fixed 9%: drizzle carves at periods 100 and 200, the same
   water in bigger helpings every 2000 steps does not, and every 20000 steps it carves again —
   because a single 1800-step burst is longer than the held threshold of 1337 and carves on its own.
   So spacing does not act monotonically, and the long-period win is the held note rather than
   burstiness. For the book: regular light rain keeps a channel that the same water in occasional
   storms does not, unless one storm is big enough to cut the channel by itself.

**Weirdness, read at each scale** (the agent's judgment, not evidence). A cell: a self-holding mark
that needs no rehearsal is ordinary, a bistable circuit or a methylation kept by its own feedback,
and the saturation the construction forced is what any real deepening has. A body: a habit that
sticks only after enough repetition, and a shorter run that fades over its own timescale, is
reasonable; what is weird is the sharpness, one step deciding, which is an artifact of one
dimension and no noise — with noise the threshold would become a probability, as in
``test_spells.py``. A society: the same shape is a practice that becomes self-funding once enough
of the town holds it, which is the commons runs' territory, and the weird part there is that
nothing in this file's cost is anybody's, so the "supply" is implicit. Across all three, the
non-reciprocal case is the warning that a tuning which carves by its own rule, rather than by the
cost the parts pay, cannot be billed to anybody.
````

### `tests/test_many_parts.py`

````
Many parts that keep one another whole: a ring and an all-to-all of parts in mutual need.

The book plan's P30 stage (e) (AGENTDOCPLAN.md §8.2 in the book repository; David,
2026-09-29, on §1.7's footnote "larger collectives are still being worked out": "then we
should do it now. we are at the point where we need to know"). The baseline is
``tests/test_mutual_need.py`` (§5.9 item 12): two parts, each a driven steady state whose
held form is supplied by the other through a gate, one supply entering from outside.

**The predictions, fixed before any construction code in
``notes/MANY-PARTS-PREDICTIONS-2026-09-29.md``.** The plan's three: (1) both wirings hold
together under the same supply condition as the pair; (2) cutting one part's supply ends
the ring, every part in turn, while all-to-all loses only that part and settles lower;
(3) the ring's collapse runs around it at a speed set by the parts' settling time. The
run's own, E1-E5, are in the note and are quoted in the docstrings below.

**The construction.** ``n`` parts, each item 12's part (``mn.part``): a configuration
coalgebra on its amplitude ``q_i`` with mobility ``h``, publishing ``q_i``, carrying its
own dissipation ``-d (b/2) q_i^2``. One supply box holding ``s`` (zero response). The
three are tensored and closed by a stateless coupler on the ports ``e = (q_1..q_n, s)``
returning

    F_i(e) = alpha ( s m_i g(r_i, q_i) + beta r_i ),     g(r, q) = r q / (K + r q),

where ``r_i`` is what part ``i`` receives from the parts it needs -- *ring*:
``r_i = q_{i-1}``; *all-to-all*: ``r_i = mean_{j != i} q_j`` -- and ``m_i`` is the supply
mask (a cut wire delivers ``0``, item 12's reading). With ``beta = 0`` a part with nothing
arriving has no drive at all, item 12's condition that no ungated path reach a part;
``beta > 0`` is that run's ungated internal coupling, run here as a control. The composite
forcing on ``Q = R^{n+1}`` is ``xi_i = F_i - b q_i``, ``xi_S = 0``, and one step is
``q + h xi``. Fixed points do not depend on ``h``.

Every docstring below says which of three things its test does (STATUS.md §3.7):
reproduce a closed form derived in the docstring, check a discrete analogue, or correct a
claim made on paper. The numbers: ``notes/MANY-PARTS-RESULTS.md``.

````
