## `tests/test_two_tanks.py`, head before 2026-10-30

```python
"""Two tanks, three couplers (book sec.two_tanks; ``background/note-retroactivity.tex`` §3).

A tank is an arrangement ``<R^0|R^0> -> <R^0|R>`` with parameter the configuration
coalgebra on its volume ``v``, response ``sharp_v = A(h(v))/c`` (its capacitance, so
its shape is encoded once), output the base pressure ``e = c h(v)``, and zero
forcing. A coupler is a *stateless* arrangement ``<R^0|R^2> -> <R^0|R^0>`` whose
ports receive the two pressures and whose forcing on ``M^+ = R^2`` is the
returned covector ``F(e)``; the closed system is
``compose_seq(tensor(tank_1, tank_2), coupler)``. Because ``out_f = c h(v)`` pulls
a port covector back by ``c h'(v) = c/A(h)`` and the response multiplies by
``A(h)/c``, the displacement is ``v' = v + F(e)`` whatever the tank's shape.

The note writes its one-forms in CDLM's sign (``dot q = -sharp omega``); the
book's forcing is the note's covector with a minus sign (STATUS.md §1),
and the coalgebra adds:

* pipe:      ``omega = -dV``, ``V = (kappa/2)((e_1 - e_2) + c z)^2``, so
             ``F = (-I, +I)``, ``I = kappa((e_1 - e_2) + c z)``; exact.
* waterfall: ``omega = -phi(e_1)(de_1 - de_2)``, ``F = (-phi(e_1), +phi(e_1))``;
             ``d omega = phi'(e_1) de_1 ^ de_2 != 0``; tank 1 never feels tank 2.
* pump:      ``omega = p (de_1 - de_2)``, ``F = (+p, -p)``; exact (a linear potential).

Reciprocity lemma (note §3): for a stateless coupler returning ``F_i(e)`` at port
``i``, ``omega = dV`` implies ``dF_i/de_j = (dF_j/de_i)^T``; in general ``d omega``
is the antisymmetric part of ``DF``. Tested here on the pipe and the waterfall.
"""
```

## `tests/test_heat_equation.py`, head before 2026-10-30

```python
"""The heat equation: the chain of harmonic particles with *configuration* parameters.

One wiring, two arrangements (book sec.heat_waves, def.arr): the chain wiring
applied to particles whose parameter is ``configuration(Q)`` is the dissipative
first-order discrete heat equation; the same wiring applied to particles with
``phase(Q)`` is the wave equation (test_wave_equation.py). There is one dynamics
functor ``Phi`` and no integrator to choose. With particle sharp ``p |-> p/m`` and
harmonic forcing ``omega = -dk``, the displacement ``q -> q + sharp_q(xi_Q)`` is
forward Euler for

    m (q_i(t+1) - q_i(t)) = kappa (q_{i-1} - 2 q_i + q_{i+1})(t),

the graph Laplacian of the chain. In the stable regime ``kappa/m`` small it
dissipates to the pinned equilibrium ``q = 0``.

The ends are held by boxes (plan P43 (b) job 4, Claude Opus 5.5, de71554a, 2026-10-10): every run
below is ``Phi`` of ``wiring.held_chain``, the chain with a held particle at ``0`` on each end, the
right one joined to the last part by a spring, closed. Until then the ends were held in code: the
run fed ``q_prev = 0`` in and computed the wall's pull ``xi_N = -kappa * q_K`` from the state each
step (CDLM seeded ``+kappa * q_K`` and its integrator subtracted; STATUS.md §1). That port-fed run is
kept as ``_step_by_port`` and checked equal to the held chain.
"""
```

## `tests/test_kirchhoff_node.py`, head before 2026-10-30

```python
"""A Kirchhoff node: the current law falls out of the wiring (P32 (e)).

The derivation under test is the book repo's ``background/note-retroactivity.tex``
l.94-103, *Kirchhoff's current law is the chain rule*: a circuit node is a capacitor
component; the wiring COPIES its voltage to ``k`` element ports; the pullback of
covectors along a copy is their SUM; hence ``q' = -sum_j I_j`` (CDLM's sign). In this
library the step adds the forcing (STATUS.md §1), so the claim reads
``Delta q = -dt sum_j I_j`` with ``I_j = (e_node - e_j)/R_j`` positive leaving the node.

Components, in the idiom of ``tests/test_two_tanks.py`` under the hydraulic-electrical
dictionary (tank = capacitor, pressure = voltage, pipe = resistor):

* capacitor ``C``: state the charge ``q``, response ``sharp = C``, output ``e = q/C``,
  zero forcing; the port covector is pulled back by ``1/C`` and the response multiplies
  by ``C``, so ``q' = q + F(e)``: the returned covector is the charge increment;
* resistor ``R``: a stateless two-port coupler, ``omega = -dV``,
  ``V = (dt/2R)(e_1 - e_2)^2``, so ``F = (-dt I, +dt I)``;
* ideal source ``E``: trivial parameter, constant output; the vjp of a constant is zero,
  so it absorbs any current;
* copy: ``fan_wire`` (``arrangements/commons.py``), ``m |-> m[picks]``, zero forcing.
  Nothing in any component sums over branches.

Predictions (``notes/P32-E-PREDICTIONS-2026-09-29.md``, fixed before this file):

1. one capacitor copied to three resistors to three sources: the ``Phi`` step's charge
   increment is ``-dt sum_j (e - E_j)/R_j`` to ``rtol 1e-12``; the closed forcing on
   ``Q`` is the same sum; two equal resistors to opposite offsets cancel exactly
   (closed form);
2. a triangle of three capacitors: the closed forcing is ``-dt L C^{-1} q`` with ``L``
   the hand-written weighted Laplacian (closed form); total charge is constant to
   ``rtol 1e-12`` at every step (discrete analogue); the voltages converge to the
   capacitance-weighted mean of the initial voltages (closed form, ``atol 1e-8``);
3. one capacitor to two sources through unequal resistors rests at the
   conductance-weighted mean of the sources, the nodal-analysis result (closed form).

What is guaranteed by construction and what is not is written in the predictions note.
"""
```
