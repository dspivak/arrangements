A coil can start another circuit ringing

Charge the first capacitor, then let go. Each coil makes a changing field; the other circuit starts ringing without a wire between them. The coils stay fixed here. Change their spacing or flip a winding to start a new experiment.

What is this a picture of?

Every wire here runs straight out of the screen, so a magnet is a long coil seen end-on: two wires, one carrying its current toward you (the dot) and one away (the cross). The thin lines are the field's lines; where they crowd, the field is strong.

The only thing entered is the field of one wire. A magnet emits the field of its two wires, and what it costs a magnet to sit in the others' field is minus half its current times the flux of that field through it. Nothing says what two magnets do to each other: the pull, the push and the turning come out of putting the parts together.

A dead loop is a closed ring with no battery. It lets no flux through, so when a magnet comes near, a current runs in it that cancels the magnet's flux, and that current pushes the magnet away.

Each wire also carries a bumper. A bumper makes a second field, steep and short, and it costs a bumper to sit in another's: that is all that keeps two things out of one place. With friction, two magnets that pull come to rest where the pull equals the bumpers' push. Without the bumpers they would fall into each other.

What was checked

The page steps the same equations, by the same Runge–Kutta rule, as the companion library's arrangement (arrangements/magnets.py). At the library's test settings the two agree to the last digit shown there: the force between two wires, the four forces between two magnets, 300 steps of two magnets closing and parting, 400 steps of a magnet turning, 1000 steps of a magnet passing a dead loop, and 6000 steps of two magnets with bumpers and friction coming to rest (viewer/models/check_magnets.js).

What the model leaves out: the field arrives at once, so there is no light; everything is per unit of length of the wires; the bumper's field is a chosen shape, not a law.
