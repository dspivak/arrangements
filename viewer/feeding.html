A receiver rides a cart along a line. Its eye sweeps how far away it looks; when the place it looks at is red, the eye holds there and the wheels drive the cart toward it. While it sees nothing red, the cart patrols: it follows a point that sweeps slowly up and down the line. A casing is red while it is full of food. At the casing the lever grips it, lifts it to the mouth, pries it open, and the receiver digests the food into its battery. Everything is paid for from the battery.

The page runs one arrangement: a receiver wired to every casing. Each casing is a source: its food, its opening, its height as a mass, gravity, the ground, and a dragger. It sends out how full it is, and so how red. The receiver holds a battery, a grip, the lever's height, the cart (a mass on a dragger whose memory is the wheel), and the eye's look distance and scan. Its stateless parts push back on what they read: the eye's motors, the wheel's drive, the gripper, the lever and the springs of the attachment. Every push on one value adds, and each store moves by the push it receives. Each motor pays the battery for what it pushes times how fast its store moves.
